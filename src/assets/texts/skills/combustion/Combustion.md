# Combustion

Combustion is the study of how fuels react with oxidizers to release
energy through chemical processes. In mechanical and aerospace
engineering, it underpins propulsion, power generation, and energy
efficiency. My coursework focused on both the **fundamental
thermochemistry of combustion** and the **computational modeling of
advanced processes**, ranging from conventional premixed flames to
detonation-driven propulsion concepts.

## What I Learned

-   Balanced **stoichiometric combustion equations** for hydrocarbon and
    alternative fuels.\
-   Applied **thermochemistry and chemical equilibrium analysis** to
    predict flame composition, flame speed, and adiabatic flame
    temperature.\
-   Analyzed **equivalence ratio and fuel--air ratio effects** on
    performance and emissions.\
-   Investigated **pollutant formation** (NOx, CO, unburned
    hydrocarbons) under various operating conditions.\
-   Explored **deflagration vs. detonation regimes**, including
    shock-driven combustion.\
-   Used **NASA CEA, MATLAB, and computational tools** to calculate
    flame properties, detonation velocities, and pressure effects.

## Core Concepts in Practice

-   **Stoichiometry & Equivalence Ratio (ϕ):** Fuel--air mixture balance
    and its role in efficiency and emissions.\
-   **Adiabatic Flame Temperature:** Maximum theoretical combustion
    temperature without heat losses.\
-   **Pollutant Formation:** Temperature and pressure effects on NOx and
    CO generation.\
-   **Combustion Regimes:**
    -   **Deflagration** -- subsonic flame propagation controlled by
        heat and mass transfer.\
    -   **Detonation** -- supersonic combustion driven by shock waves,
        producing rapid energy release.\
-   **Detonation Theory:**
    -   ZND (Zel'dovich--von Neumann--Döring) model for detonation
        structure.\
    -   Chapman--Jouguet (CJ) conditions for detonation velocity and
        pressure.\
    -   Applications to **pulse detonation engines** and **rotating
        detonation combustors**.
