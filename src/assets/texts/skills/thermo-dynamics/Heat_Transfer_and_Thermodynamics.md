# Heat Transfer and Thermodynamics

This project gave me the chance to bring together everything I've
learned in thermodynamics and heat transfer and apply it to a real-world
energy problem: designing a geothermal **Ideal Rankine Cycle**. Using
**ammonia as the working fluid**, I built and optimized a power cycle
capable of producing 5 MW of net power while staying within strict
environmental limits on waste heat released back into a river.

What I liked most about this project was the balance it required ---
pushing efficiency while respecting sustainability. It wasn't just about
squeezing out more power; it was about finding the operating point where
engineering, environment, and practicality meet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Did

-   Modeled an **Ideal Rankine Cycle** with realistic evaporator and
    condenser constraints, assuming isentropic compression and
    expansion.\
-   Performed **state-point analysis** on P--h and T--s diagrams for
    ammonia to map the cycle and verify assumptions.\
-   Ran **parametric studies** in EES (Engineering Equation Solver) to
    see how efficiency shifted with changes in evaporator pressure,
    condenser pressure, and mass flow rate.\
-   Integrated **heat exchanger design limits (UA values)** to account
    for real-world transfer rates and prevent excessive thermal
    pollution into the river.\
-   Generated cycle plots and performance curves that tied the
    thermodynamic theory to measurable system behavior.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Core Concepts in Action

-   **Thermodynamics:** Applied Rankine cycle principles, efficiency
    optimization, and enthalpy--entropy analysis.\
-   **Heat Transfer:** Designed around heat exchanger performance, ΔT
    limitations, and realistic UA constraints.\
-   **Sustainability:** Balanced power generation with environmental
    responsibility by minimizing river reinjection temperature.\
-   **Simulation & Tools:** Used EES to link all of this together into a
    computational model that could be optimized.
