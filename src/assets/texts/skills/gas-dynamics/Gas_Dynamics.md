# Gas Dynamics

Gas Dynamics is the study of compressible fluid flow, where changes in
density, pressure, and temperature cannot be neglected. In mechanical
and aerospace engineering, it is essential for analyzing propulsion
systems, jet and rocket engines, supersonic/hypersonic vehicles, and
high-speed thermal processes. For me, this coursework was about bridging
thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, and heat transfer into a unified
framework to model real-world high-speed flows.

## What I Learned

-   Developed governing equations for compressible flows, including the
    **continuity, momentum, and energy equations**.\
-   Analyzed **isentropic, shock, and expansion wave relations** using
    symbolic derivations and numerical computation.\
-   Applied **Mach number analysis** to understand subsonic, transonic,
    and supersonic regimes.\
-   Used **gas tables, MATLAB, and NASA CEA outputs** to evaluate flow
    properties, nozzle performance, and adiabatic flame temperatures.\
-   Modeled **choked flow, shock waves, and nozzle flows** with
    computational simulations to predict performance in propulsion
    systems.

## Core Concepts in Practice

-   **Compressibility:** Understanding when density variations drive
    flow behavior.\
-   **Flow regimes:**
    -   Subsonic (M \< 1) --- dominated by pressure and viscous
        effects.\
    -   Transonic (M ≈ 1) --- highly nonlinear region with mixed wave
        phenomena.\
    -   Supersonic (M \> 1) --- characterized by shocks and expansions.\
-   **Shocks and expansions:** How sudden and smooth pressure changes
    shape flow fields.\
-   **Nozzles and diffusers:** Designing area variations to accelerate
    or decelerate flow efficiently.\
-   **Thermochemistry:** Using CEA to analyze combustion gases,
    equivalence ratios, and flame temperatures.
