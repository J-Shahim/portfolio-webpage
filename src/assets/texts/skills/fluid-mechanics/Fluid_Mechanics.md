# Fluid Mechanics

Fluid Mechanics is the study of how liquids and gases move, how they
exert forces, and how they respond to energy and pressure changes. This
subject provided me with the ability to **analyze, design, and predict
the behavior of fluid systems** ranging from small-scale piping networks
to large-scale aerospace and energy applications.

The course emphasized not only the mathematics of conservation laws but
also the physical intuition needed to understand flow patterns, energy
transfer, and the role of viscosity and turbulence in engineering
systems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What I Did

-   Applied **continuity, momentum, and energy conservation equations**
    to describe fluid systems.\
-   Used **Bernoulli's equation** to analyze trade-offs between
    velocity, pressure, and elevation.\
-   Classified flows as **laminar or turbulent** using Reynolds number
    and transition criteria.\
-   Studied **internal flows** such as pipe systems, including head
    losses, friction factors, and energy-grade lines.\
-   Analyzed **external flows** around objects to evaluate drag, lift,
    and boundary layer behavior.\
-   Calculated **pump and turbine performance**, including efficiency,
    head, and system matching.\
-   Explored **dimensional analysis** and similarity principles for
    scaling laboratory results to real-world designs.\
-   Considered **compressible flows**, including Mach number effects,
    shock waves, and flow choking in nozzles and diffusers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Core Concepts in Action

-   **Conservation Laws:** Mass, momentum, and energy conservation as
    the foundation of all fluid problems.\
-   **Flow Regimes:** Laminar vs. turbulent flow, transitional behavior,
    and how viscosity shapes velocity profiles.\
-   **Pipe Systems:** Head loss due to friction and fittings, minor
    losses, and energy balancing across networks.\
-   **Fluid Machinery:** Pumps, turbines, compressors, and fans as
    devices for energy transfer.\
-   **Boundary Layers:** Velocity gradients at surfaces, shear stress,
    separation, and their role in drag and lift.\
-   **Compressible Flow:** Subsonic, transonic, and supersonic regimes,
    with applications in nozzles, diffusers, and high-speed
    aerodynamics.\
-   **Dimensional Analysis:** Using Buckingham Pi theorem and similarity
    to reduce complexity and guide design scaling.
